# Content Safety And Originality

## Originality

Do not copy another account's card text or layout directly.

Safe transformation means adding at least several of:

- New angle
- New Korean framing
- Different card order
- Original commentary
- Added context
- Different design system
- Different final question
- Connection to the user's recipe/blog strategy

## News And Current Claims

Browse and cite sources when the post depends on current facts:

- Food news
- Product launches
- Prices
- Rankings
- Platform rules
- Health/safety claims
- Brand controversies
- Statistics

If a fact is not verified, mark it as `확인 필요`.

## Accuracy First

Trust is more important than speed. For food news, trend, and culture posts, never present an uncertain claim as fact.

Use this process before finalizing:

1. Is this a fact, context, or opinion?
2. Does it include a number, ranking, price, date, brand claim, expert quote, or health claim?
3. Is the source current and credible?
4. If not verified, can the wording be softened?
5. If it cannot be verified or softened, remove it.

## Fact / Context / Opinion Format

Use this thinking internally and expose it when useful:

```text
Fact: verified source-backed information.
Context: why the fact matters to food culture, trends, or consumers.
Opinion: the creator's interpretation or takeaway.
```

Example:

```text
Fact: Several Korean chefs in New York are collaborating around Korean ingredients and farming.
Context: This reflects growing interest in ingredient-level Korean food culture abroad.
Opinion: Korean food content is moving beyond restaurants and recipes into farming, fermentation, and sourcing stories.
```

## Claims That Need Sources

Always verify or avoid:

- Sales numbers
- Market size
- Rankings
- "No.1" or "most popular"
- Prices
- Launch dates
- Brand statements
- Ingredient origin claims
- Food safety claims
- Health, diet, nutrition, or child-related claims
- "Going viral worldwide"
- "Everyone is eating this"
- Expert quotes
- Legal or platform policy claims

## Safer Wording

Use softer wording when a trend is observed but not fully verified.

Risky:

```text
전 세계에서 난리 난 간식
```

Safer:

```text
SNS에서 자주 보이는 간식 트렌드
```

Risky:

```text
미국에서 1위한 한국 디저트
```

Safer:

```text
미국 푸드 콘텐츠에서 자주 언급되는 한국 디저트
```

Risky:

```text
건강에 좋은 발효 음식
```

Safer:

```text
발효 방식으로 만들어지는 음식으로 알려져 있습니다
```

## Source Display

For news/trend posts, include sources in the caption or final card when appropriate.

Example:

```text
Sources: Bon Appétit, brand press release, Google Trends
출처: Bon Appétit, 브랜드 공식 자료, Google Trends
```

## Copyright And Rights

Avoid using copyrighted photos, celebrity photos, logos, screenshots, or another creator's post in a way that implies ownership.

Better options:

- User-provided original photos
- User-created graphics
- Licensed/official press images when allowed
- AI-generated illustrative images clearly used as illustration
- Public-domain/usable sources with attribution when required

## Risky Claims To Avoid

- Health benefits without evidence
- Diet/weight loss claims
- Child growth or brain-development claims
- "No.1", "best", "guaranteed", "100%"
- Fake expert authority
- Fake personal experience
- Fake discounts, scarcity, or sales numbers

## Engagement Safety

Comment prompts are fine when they invite genuine opinion or memory.

Good:

- "당신의 추억 간식은 뭐였나요?"
- "이 중에 제일 다시 먹고 싶은 건?"
- "요즘 다시 유행했으면 하는 간식은?"

Avoid:

- Asking people to comment only to manipulate engagement
- Misleading giveaways
- False promises in exchange for comments
